# Stage 4–5: feedback architecture and educational assessment

## Interactive causal-loop diagram

The `feedbacks.html` module makes the five causal hypotheses explicit:

- R1 — infrastructure lock-in;
- R2 — accumulated technological advantage;
- R3 — economic/institutional dependence;
- B1 — climate-policy response;
- B2 — alternative-technology substitution.

Users can isolate a loop or select a policy lever. The diagram then highlights the parts of the causal structure through which the intervention is represented.

The diagram is a structural hypothesis map, not a calibrated causal graph. Link polarity means the direction of change, not a moral or policy judgment.

## Educational assessment

The `assessment.html` module is a voluntary self-assessment, not a research study.

It includes six questions covering:

1. stock-flow accumulation;
2. policy transmission and delay;
3. resources versus climate budget;
4. path dependence and capital turnover;
5. extraction versus atmospheric emission;
6. natural carbon sinks.

### Privacy design

The assessment:

- requests no name, email, identifier, or demographic information;
- sends no responses to a server;
- does not call a network API;
- does not use localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, or another persistent browser store;
- keeps pre-check answers only in JavaScript page memory;
- reveals correct answers only after the post-check;
- labels results as educational feedback, not validated research findings.

A future controlled learning study would require a separate research protocol, validated measurement design, consent/privacy procedures, and any applicable institutional ethics review before participant data collection is enabled.
